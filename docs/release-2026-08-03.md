## What was built

A French photography portfolio for Irina Adamenko (Lyon), replacing a Wix-era site. Next.js
App Router, TypeScript, Tailwind v4, deployed on Vercel; content is typed data in the
repository, not a CMS. Two independently-authored locales, an inquiry-only conversion model
(no e-commerce, no booking engine), and a build pipeline that turns the photographer's own
exports into the published portfolio.

**Surfaces:** homepage · galleries index · four genre walls · sixteen story pages with a
lightbox exhibition · four service dossiers · pricing · about · contact · legal · 404.

---

## Major architectural decisions

**Content is typed data, not a CMS (D009).** Every gallery, price and legal fact is a
TypeScript module. Adding a photograph is one entry plus one export — reviewable in a diff,
impossible to half-publish.

**The story pipeline is the source of record.** `story.txt` in the photographer's own library
is the edit — which frames, in which order, with what alt text, and whether the story is
public at all. `stories-build.mjs` publishes it; the generated model is never hand-edited.
A folder being found on disk is never consent to publish it (`visibility` defaults to draft).

**Exports carry a content hash — `<slug>-NN.<hash8>.jpg`.** Position-only names meant that
removing a frame renumbered everything after it, so a URL silently came to mean a *different
photograph*. With a 31-day image cache that kept a withdrawn frame online at its recycled
URL. A URL now denotes one photograph for all time.

**CHAMBRE design language.** One dark room: obsidian surface, film grain, vignette, a
floating glass header, and prints that develop out of underexposure as they enter. Motion is
composited-only (opacity/transform) and opt-in behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Native platform over libraries.** The lightbox and mobile menu are native `<dialog>`
elements — focus trap, Escape, top layer and focus return come from the platform, not from
a dependency.

**Locale routing is one system.** `link()` and the hreflang/canonical builder emit the same
localized URLs; the proxy rewrites `/en/<alias>` to the internal route.

## Gallery editing summary

The photographer's library was cut from **987 frames to 492** across sixteen stories.

| Genre | Stories | Frames |
|---|---|---|
| Couples | 4 | 26 · 35 · 35 · 27 |
| Familles | 4 | 33 · 44 · 39 · 27 |
| Grossesse | 4 | 21 · 25 · 21 · 25 |
| Mariages | 4 | 15 · 38 · 38 · 43 |

**Consent and decency drove the largest cuts.** One family séance was withdrawn entirely
(`visibility: private`; exports deleted from the web root) because permission had not been
given. The maternity séance `grossesse-1` was re-cut repeatedly against a rule the
photographer set — any frame in which the garment was see-through was removed — ending at 21
frames after a full re-review of all 47 source frames.

**`mariages-1` is short (15) on purpose.** Re-reviewed 2026-08-02: the original folder holds
only 23 byte-identical files, and seven of the eight unpublished frames are alternates or
mono/colour twins of moments already on the wall; the eighth has a crew member in it.
Nothing to restore without showing the same photograph twice.

**Covers are chosen for the crop they will actually get.** `couples-3`'s cover was replaced
after the 21:9 lead plate was found cutting the couple's heads; the replacement was picked by
rendering every candidate through the real centre-crop.

Every dropped frame is `#`-commented in its `story.txt` with the reason, so the edit explains
itself to the next person who opens it.

## Performance

Measured on a throttled mobile profile (390×844@3×, 4× CPU), dropped frames read from the
compositor's frame pipeline — main-thread rAF deltas cannot see raster cost.

| Work | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Exhibition scroll — `mariages-4` | 318 dropped | **114** (−64%) |
| Exhibition scroll — `couples-3` | 308 | **115** (−63%) |
| Exhibition scroll — `familles-3` | 358 | **169** (−53%) |
| Genre-page LCP load delay | 81 ms | **4 ms** |

**Cause of the scroll jank:** the tile reveal animated `filter: blur(7px)`. `opacity` and
`transform` composite; `blur()` forces re-rasterisation every frame, at full width, on
dozens of photographs at once. Removing the blur (keeping the brightness ramp, which carries
the meaning) fixed it.

**LCP:** each page's opening print now loads like the LCP element it is — `priority` plus an
explicit `fetchPriority` (Next 16 no longer implies one from the other). CLS is **0.00** on
every page, in every engine, at every viewport.

*Ruled out by measurement, not assumption:* scroll handlers (all already passive and
rAF-coalesced, zero long tasks), layout thrashing, forced reflow (29 ms total, mostly the
profiler itself), `backdrop-filter`, the ambient layer, grain/vignette, and blur-up
placeholders — each disabled in isolation, none changed anything.

## Accessibility

**Lighthouse accessibility 100** on every template. **Zero axe WCAG 2.1 A/AA violations**
across Chromium · Firefox · WebKit at 390 / 768 / 1440.

Fixed in the final cycle, all found by testing states page-load tooling cannot reach:

- **Contact form error text at 3.00:1** (needs 4.5). The light-surface `--color-error` token
  was never re-pointed when the site went dark. Now 5.11:1. Only visible after a failed
  submit, which is why Lighthouse scored the page 100.
- **Pricing card duration label at 2.03:1** — styled with the *disabled-control* token on
  active content. Now 5.23:1.
- **Lightbox swipe-down-to-dismiss was dead on every touch device** — `touch-action: pan-y`
  handed vertical panning to the browser, so `pointerup` never fired.

Also verified: native-dialog focus trap and focus return to the exact trigger; live region
announcing lightbox changes; skip link; one `<h1>` and a correct `lang` per page; French
legal documents served under `/en` tagged `lang="fr"` so a screen reader switches voice.

# OPTIONAL IMPROVEMENTS

Real value, none of it blocking, none of it urgent.

1. **Editorial alt text.** All 492 frames carry a positional placeholder (`«Titre» — 7 / 22`).
   It is consistent and technically valid, but describes nothing to a screen reader or to
   Google Images. ~1 000 lines; wants a plan rather than a sitting. **Do this before any
   image sitemap** — a sitemap over placeholder alt text is worth nothing.
2. **Image sitemap** — after (1), not before.
3. **Google Places Actions secrets** (`GOOGLE_PLACES_API_KEY`, `GOOGLE_PLACE_ID`) to
   reactivate automated review sync. The pipeline is built and idle.
4. **Hi-res masters.** Exports are capped at 2200px on the long edge. At 1440@2× the lead
   plate delivers 0.94× the pixels it could use — a 6% shortfall, not visible, but a larger
   master would close it.
5. **`EN` link over the homepage hero** sits at 1.99:1. Cannot be fixed by recolouring — the
   backdrop is bright enough that even white gives 3.0. Needs a darker scrim or a different
   hero: a visual call, not a defect to patch.
6. **Structured data `@graph`.** Entities are currently isolated nodes rather than one linked
   graph. Valid as-is; `@graph` would express the relationships.
7. **EN legal pages are French.** Correctly tagged `lang="fr"` so assistive tech and search
   engines are told the truth. Translating them is a decision for the operator and a French
   legal professional, not an engineering task.
8. **`mariages-1` at 15 frames** vs siblings at 38–43. Only resolvable with frames that were
   never delivered — a question for the photographer's archive.
9. **`grossesse-1` description says "une journée en noir et blanc"** while the edit is about
   half colour. Either the description or the edit is out of step; both readings are
   defensible and the choice is the photographer's.
